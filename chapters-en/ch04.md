# Chapter 4: Your Footprints

## The Data You Give Away Yourself

So far we have talked about stolen data. But there is one kind of data that is not stolen. You give it away yourself. Laughing, having fun.

Before we start the story, let us clear up a few words. Cookie, tracker, device fingerprinting, reverse image search. These words will not scare you, because now you will understand them in simple language.

### Cookie: The Shopkeeper's Memory

A cookie is a tiny file that a website places in your phone or computer.

Take an example. You go to the same tea stall every day. The shopkeeper starts recognizing you. "The same strong tea." He remembers what you like. A website's cookie does the same work. When you open that site again, it remembers who you are, what you looked at, what your language is.

A cookie is not bad by itself. Most of the useful things on the internet, like staying logged in or keeping items in your shopping cart, work because of cookies. The problem starts when cookies stop being useful and start being nosy. You look at shoes on one site, and then ads for those same shoes start showing on every other site. That is the work of third-party cookies. That means one company's cookie is following you on other sites too.

### Trackers and Pixels: The Invisible Spies

Trackers are small programs hidden inside websites. They watch what you clicked, how long you stayed, where you came from. And the whole game happens silently, in the background, without you knowing anything.

A pixel is even smaller. It is an invisible one-pixel image hidden in an email or a webpage. The moment you open that page, the pixel informs its owner. "This person has opened the email."

Understand it with an example. You stepped into a shop and the shopkeeper secretly put a man behind you to note down everything you looked at. Trackers and pixels do the same work in the digital world. You cannot see them, but every move is noted.

### Device Fingerprinting: Your Phone's Identity

Your phone also has its own separate identity. Screen size, operating system, browser version, installed fonts, time zone. Put all of these together and a unique identity is formed. This is called device fingerprinting.

Just like every person's fingerprints are different, every device's digital mark is different. You can delete cookies, you can go into private mode, still a website can recognize your device.

Ad companies use this technique. And the bad news is that avoiding it is harder than deleting cookies.

### Reverse Image Search: From Photo to Person

Reverse image search means putting in a photo and finding that person or place.

In a normal search, you type words and get photos. In reverse search, you give a photo and get information. From just one of your photos, someone can find your name, your old profile, your city's address.

Fraudsters use this too. They pick up someone's photo, do a reverse search, find their social media profile. From there, name, family, city. Then they win trust with that same information and cheat.

### Imagine... (This Story Is Fictional)

Imagine there is a man, let us call him Ramesh. Ramesh is 35 years old. He lives in Delhi. Now let us go through his routine the way a stranger on the internet could see it.

Last month Ramesh posted a photo of his son's birthday on Facebook. In the caption he wrote, "My son turned 8 today." The photo shows the hall of his house. Behind, there is a certificate hanging on the wall, on which Ramesh's full name can be clearly read.

Ramesh goes for a morning walk in the park every day. He posts photos of it on Instagram, with the location tag. Now anyone knows that Ramesh is not at home from 6 to 7 in the morning.

Ramesh once played that "what will your future be like" quiz. That app took his friend list, his likes, his birth date, everything.

Ramesh posted a photo of his new car. The number plate is clearly visible.

Now tell me. Ramesh's full name, his son's age, his home, his daily routine, his car's number. All of this can become known to a stranger. Without meeting Ramesh, without hacking his phone. All of this came from things Ramesh himself posted, happily, in the open.

This story is fictional. But this method is completely real. It is called OSINT. Open Source Intelligence. That means joining together openly available information to build someone's complete picture.

### The Price of Free Things

You have heard that saying. If something is being given for free, then the real product is you.

Remember the fun quizzes on Facebook. "What will you become in your next birth." "How sharp is your mind." To play these quizzes, you press a button. "Login with Facebook." And in that one click, you hand over the key to your profile to that app.

In 2018, the world's biggest data scandal came out. There was a company, Cambridge Analytica. Through a quiz app made by a researcher, "thisisyourdigitallife", it took the data of Facebook users. Not only of those who played the quiz, but of their friends too. In total, profiles of about 8.7 crore people. This data was used to target voters. (Source: Financial Express)

Just one quiz. And the data of 8.7 crore people.

### The Runners Opened Up the Army's Bases

Now a true incident that shows how dangerous even innocent-looking information can be.

Strava is a fitness app. People record their running and cycling routes in it. In 2017, the company released a global heatmap. That means it showed the routes of users from all over the world on one map. Wherever more people ran, the map glowed there.

In January 2018, a student from Australia, Nathan Ruser, saw something strange. In the desert of Syria, where there is almost no population, running routes were glowing. On looking closely, it turned out these routes were of the military bases of America and its allied countries.

Soldiers would go running after their duty and switch on the app. From their routes, the outer wall of the bases, the patrol path, even the inside map came out in the open. (Source: TechCrunch)

The American defense department had to review it. Later Strava changed its settings. But the lesson is clear. Your fitness information can also become spying information for someone.

You are not a soldier, but your daily walk's route can also tell someone when you are not at home. Do check the privacy settings of your fitness app.

### Profiles of 70 Crore People Sold

In 2021, another big news came. A hacker claimed that he had the data of LinkedIn's 70 crore users. Name, email, phone number, job information, address. This data was put up for sale on the dark web.

LinkedIn said this was not a new theft. This data was scraped from their site. That means the hacker automatically collected the openly visible information from their website. According to the company, no private data was leaked. (Source: Computer Weekly)

But the truth is that whether it is scraped or stolen, the result is the same. The information of 70 crore people gathered in one place and was being sold. And this information you yourself had put in your profile.

One thing becomes clear from this. The information you put in the open does not stay yours. It can become part of any list.

### The Pizza Lesson

In the last chapter we talked about the Domino's search portal. Remember it once more.

A hacker had made a website where anyone could enter someone's mobile number and see their order history. People used it to spy on others. Where someone's girlfriend lives. Where someone's boss orders food from. (Source: MediaNama)

This data was hacked and stolen. But what happened after that was OSINT. The use of openly available information.

### What Is OSINT

OSINT is not hacking. In this, nobody's phone is broken into, no password is stolen.

OSINT means joining together information scattered in the open. Your Facebook post, your Instagram location, your old tweets, your name on some website. Put all of these together and the picture of your life is ready.

The police also use OSINT. Journalists too. And criminals too. The only difference is the intention.

Fraudsters also use OSINT in frauds like digital arrest. They first collect information about you. Your name, your city, your family members' names. Then they say this same information on the phone and win your trust. You feel that the person in front is a government officer, because he knows everything about you. In reality, he had picked up all of this from your social media itself.

### How to Reduce Your Footprints

Now the good news. You can reduce your own footprints yourself.

First, open the privacy settings of your social media. Today itself. See who can see your posts. Change "public" to "friends".

Second, turn off location tags. There is no need to tell your location with every photo. Especially never the location of your home and your children's school.

Third, stay away from quizzes and fun apps. Remove at once any app that asks for your friend list.

Fourth, search yourself on Google. Search your name, your number, your email. Whatever shows up, try to get it removed.

Fifth, be careful while posting children's photos. The school's name, the uniform, the location. It is better if none of these are visible.

Sixth, put your photo in reverse image search and see where it appears. If your photo shows up on some unknown site, demand that it be removed.

Seventh, check the settings of your fitness app and location-sharing apps. Put your home and office in "private zones" so that your route is not visible to everyone.

### WiFi and Bluetooth Also Leave Marks

One last thing that few people know. Your phone does not leave marks only on the internet.

When your WiFi or Bluetooth stays on, your phone keeps sending signals to the networks around it. "I am here." Sensors placed in malls, airports, and big shops catch these signals. From this, it becomes known when you came, how long you stayed, which shop you went to.

To avoid this, keep WiFi and Bluetooth off when not needed. Especially in crowded places. This small habit reduces your digital marks a lot.

**In one line:** A thief does not need to break the lock when you keep the door open yourself, so keep the door of your data closed.
