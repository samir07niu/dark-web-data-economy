# Chapter 2: The Door to Get Inside

## The Game of Hiding Your Identity

In the last chapter you read the name Ross Ulbricht. The owner of Silk Road, the dark web's biggest market. He thought Tor would save him. But he was arrested in a public library.

The question is, how did he get caught even after having such a strong technology like Tor.

The answer is in one word. Mistakes. His own mistakes.

In this chapter we will first clear some important words. VPN, encryption, metadata, node. These words come again and again in news. Understand them in simple language, then the stories will feel even more interesting.

### Encryption: The Locked Box

Encryption means changing information into such a code that only the one with the right key can read it.

Take an example. You write a letter and close it in a locked box. On the way anyone can lift that box, shake it, but cannot know what is written inside. The key is only with the one who gets it.

The same happens in the digital world. Your message changes into a code before it is sent. Even if someone catches it in the middle, they will only see useless letters. In the receiver's phone it becomes the real message again.

Encryption in itself is a good thing. Your banking, your WhatsApp messages, all are safe because of this. Criminals also use it so that no one can read their talks.

### Metadata: The Address Written Outside the Letter

Now a fine point. The letter is in the locked box. But what is written outside the box. The sender's name, the receiver's address, the date, the weight. All this is seen from outside itself.

This is called metadata. That means data about data. What your message is can be hidden, but when you sent the message, to whom you sent it, how big you sent it. This information is often not hidden.

For the police, metadata is like gold. They do not need to know what the talk was. They only need to know who was talking to whom, when and how many times. From this itself the whole net opens up.

### Node: The Post Offices on the Way

In the last chapter we read the onion example. Your letter goes through three places in three envelopes.

Those three places are called nodes or relays. A node is a computer that passes your traffic forward. The first node only knows that the traffic came from you. The last node only knows where the traffic has to go. The middle node does not know even the two ends.

These nodes are the computers of common people across the world. Someone in Germany, someone in Brazil, someone in Japan. That is why catching any one of them does not open the whole path.

### What Is the Tor Browser

The Tor Browser is a special browser that sends your internet traffic with the onion technique. We understood this in the last chapter. Three layers, three computers, no one knows who the sender is.

Let me make one thing clear. The Tor Browser is not a crime by itself. Journalists across the world use it. In some countries where governments watch the internet, ordinary people use it too. It is a privacy tool.

But like every tool, it is also misused. Criminals use it so that the police cannot reach their real location.

### The Difference Between VPN and Tor

Many people think VPN and Tor are the same. Both hide identity, but the way is completely different.

Understand VPN like this. You leave your home and through a tunnel reach another city. Now the world feels you came from that other city. But the company that runs that tunnel knows where you came from.

That means in VPN your real address is known to the VPN company. If that company gives that information to the police or its record gets stolen, then your identity can open up.

In Tor there is no one company. Your traffic passes through three different nodes and no one node knows the full picture. That is why Tor hides identity more strongly than VPN. But Tor is slow and every website does not run properly on it.

One more thing. VPN is legal and ordinary people also use it. Office work, safety on public WiFi. But remember, VPN does not make you fully disappear. It only changes your address.

### What Is Tails OS

Criminals take the name of one more thing. Tails OS. It is a special operating system that runs from a pen drive. The moment you switch off the computer, its full record gets wiped. No files left, no traces left.

I am telling you this so that you do not panic on hearing the name, not so that you use it. An ordinary person does not need these things. This information is only so that you know what is going on at the other side.

### OPSEC Means Not Making Your Own Mistake

In the world of criminals, one word is used a lot. OPSEC. Its full name is Operational Security.

In simple words it means this. Do not make any mistake linked to your identity. Different names for different work, different emails, different habits. Never let your real life and your hidden life meet.

Sounds easy to hear. But in reality it is very hard. Humans make mistakes. And in the digital world, even one small mistake gets recorded forever.

### First Mistake: His Real Email

Ross Ulbricht started Silk Road. In the beginning he had to tell people about it. So he wrote a post on a forum with the name "Altoid".

Then with the same "Altoid" name he asked for help at another place. And this time he wrote his real email. rossulbricht@gmail.com.

That is it. Game over.

The investigators matched this email with Google. They found his photo, his social media accounts. They also found his question on a programming forum, where he had asked a technical question about Tor. The same code was later found on Silk Road's server. (Source: CoinDesk)

Tor could not save him, because Tor cannot hide his mistake. He had told his real identity himself.

### Second Mistake: The Welcome Email

Now listen to the second story. There was another dark web market named AlphaBay. Bigger than Silk Road. Its owner was Alexandre Cazes.

He made a mistake that makes you both laugh and wonder. When a new user made an account on AlphaBay, they got a welcome email. And the sender address of that email was Pimp_Alex_91@hotmail.com.

That was his own personal email.

The investigating agencies followed this email. They found his name, his date of birth, his LinkedIn profile. When the police in Thailand raided his house, he was logged in to AlphaBay as admin on his laptop. There was no encryption on the laptop either. (Source: Fast Company)

In 2017 AlphaBay shut down. Cazes was arrested.

### Third Story: The Police Itself Started Running the Market

Now the most shocking story. This is of the summer of 2017.

As AlphaBay shut down, its thousands of users ran towards another market. The biggest name was Hansa Market. The users felt they had found a new shelter.

But they did not know that Hansa was now being run by the police.

What happened was this. The Netherlands police had arrested both admins of Hansa in Germany. Then on 20 June 2017, with the court's permission, they took Hansa's servers under their control. And then for about one month, till 20 July, the police itself ran that market. The users did not even get a hint. (Source: The Register)

In this one month, what all did the police do. They recorded users' passwords in plain letters. They made arrangements to read messages. They pulled out location information from photos. And they collected the addresses of about 10,000 buyers. (Source: The Register)

The users who ran from AlphaBay had jumped straight into the police's net. This was called Operation Bayonet. America's FBI, Europe's Europol and the police of many countries had done it together. (Source: Wikipedia)

This story tells a bitter truth. The thing you think is a market on the dark web, maybe it is a police checkpost.

### How the Police Catch Them

One thing is clear from these three stories. The police often do not catch them by breaking Tor. They catch human mistakes.

The first way is the money trail. Criminals use cryptocurrency like Bitcoin. But when they spend that money in the real world, they leave traces.

The second way is old records. The internet forgets nothing. A ten-year-old forum post also helps in an investigation.

The third way is insiders. In many big cases, the police planted their own men inside the market or turned a caught criminal into an informer. The Hansa story is the biggest proof of this. There the police took the whole market in its own hands.

The fourth way is a server mistake. Sometimes criminals make a wrong setting while setting up a server, which leaks the real address.

### What It Means for You

You must be thinking, what do I get from knowing all this. I am not a criminal.

It means this, that no matter how strong the technology is, a human mistake is the biggest weakness. This applies to criminals and to you too.

Criminals get caught by making one mistake. And ordinary people get cheated by making one mistake. One click on a wrong link, one wrong permission to an app, one same password everywhere.

For you there are three lessons. First, keep your real identity and your online identity separate. Second, do not take metadata lightly. What you are sending can be hidden, but when and to whom you are sending is often seen. Third, VPN or any tool does not make you fully safe. Real safety is in your habits.

In the next chapter we will see where these criminals are going now, leaving the dark web. The answer will shock you. They are right inside your phone.

For you there are four lessons. First, keep your real identity and your online identity separate. Use different emails for different work. Second, do not take metadata lightly. What you are sending can be hidden, but when and to whom you are sending is often seen. Third, VPN or any tool does not make you fully safe. Real safety is in your habits. Fourth, never take old posts lightly. What you wrote ten years ago can even today be used against you in some investigation.

**In one line:** Even the best technology to hide does not work when a person tells his own identity himself, and the same applies to your safety.
